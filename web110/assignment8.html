One activity I completed was reviewing Internet Security and learning about the different threats that can affect people while using the Internet. I learned that Internet security helps protect communication, data, and Internet-connected systems. Some of the threats discussed included malware, ransomware, spam, hacking, and phishing. I found phishing especially important because scammers can make emails and websites look like they are from legitimate companies in order to steal information such as passwords or credit card numbers. I also learned that security protocols can help protect privacy, integrity, and authentication. I can apply this knowledge to my everyday Internet use by being more careful about suspicious emails, links, and websites and by thinking twice before sharing personal information online. Another activity I completed was practicing web browser safety. I learned that browsers have privacy and security settings that can help protect personal information and control what information websites store. I also learned why keeping a browser and its add-ons updated is important because outdated software can create security risks. Cookies and website data can be useful for remembering information, but they can also be used to track activity. I learned that private or incognito browsing can prevent certain information from being saved locally during a browsing session, although it does not make someone completely anonymous online. I can apply this knowledge by regularly checking my browser settings, removing unnecessary add-ons, and being more aware of the information my browser stores. A third activity I completed was researching password managers and multi-factor authentication. I learned that password managers can securely organize passwords and make it easier to use different, stronger passwords for different accounts. I also learned that multi-factor authentication adds another layer of protection because a password alone is not enough to access an account. This is especially important for accounts containing sensitive information. I can apply these concepts to my own accounts, including my school, email, social media, and financial accounts. Using stronger passwords and multi-factor authentication can help reduce the chance of someone gaining access to my accounts.
