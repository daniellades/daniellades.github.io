Assignment 2

HTML5 Accessibility

The first activity I completed was about HTML5 Accessibility. It involved comparing how different web browsers work with accessibility tools. I learned that browsers do not all support accessibility features in the same way. Edge had the highest score at 100%, while Chrome and Opera scored 98.5%, Safari scored 97%, Firefox scored 94%, and Internet Explorer scored 56%. I learned that a website can work normally but still be difficult for people who use screen readers or only a keyboard. This showed me that accessibility is an important part of building a website. In the future, if I ever create a website or collaborate on a project, I want to make sure it is easy for everyone to use, not just people who can use a mouse or see the screen.

Comparing Web Browsers

The second activity, Comparing Web Browsers, was about comparing browsers such as Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Brave, and Opera. I learned that browsers may look similar, but they have different features and are designed for different purposes. Some focus more on speed, while others focus on privacy or convenience. For example, Brave automatically blocks many ads and trackers, while other browsers may require extensions to do this. I learned that choosing a browser depends on what is most important to the user. I can use this knowledge in the future by choosing browsers based on my needs and using different browsers when I need to test websites or keep certain accounts separate.

Browser Settings

The third activity, Browser Settings, was interesting because I learned about the different settings available in web browsers. I learned that settings for cookies, tracking, saved passwords, payments, camera access, microphone access, and location can affect my privacy and security. Some of these settings can be found in the advanced settings area. I learned that it is important to understand what permissions websites have instead of accepting every default setting. I can apply this in my own life by checking my browser settings regularly and making sure websites only have access to information I am comfortable sharing.

Brave Browser

The fourth activity I focused on was the Brave Browser. I had already used it before this class because I hoped it could protect me from viruses I might encounter while watching TV online, and I have had a good experience with it. It was interesting to look into it in more detail. I learned that Brave focuses strongly on privacy and security by automatically blocking many ads and trackers. This can also help websites load faster. Brave also gives users control over cookies, notifications, and website permissions. I learned that privacy features can be built directly into a browser instead of requiring extra extensions. This knowledge is useful when I want more privacy and helps me become more aware of how different browsers protect my information.
